01 — The idea

Made in the open, run on your desk.

Most tools that turn a video into images ask you to hand the video over first. The file goes up, something happens on a machine you cannot see, and a ZIP comes back. For a holiday clip that is merely inconvenient. For an unreleased cut, a medical scan, a client interview or a screen recording of your own dashboard, it is a decision you should not have to make.

FrameByFrame does the work where the file already is. Your browser has a video decoder and a canvas; that is genuinely all frame extraction needs. Load the page once and extraction keeps working with the network switched off, because there is no server in the loop to talk to.

02 — How it works

Four steps. No sign-up.

From a file on your desktop to a folder of numbered stills, without an account or an upload.

  1. 01

    Load the video

    Drag a file anywhere onto the page, browse for it, or paste it from the clipboard. FrameByFrame reads the duration, native dimensions and true frame rate, measuring the rate from real presentation timestamps rather than assuming 30fps.

  2. 02

    Find your range

    Scrub the filmstrip timeline, step one frame at a time, and set in and out points to bracket the part you care about. Everything downstream respects that range, so a two-second moment in a two-hour recording costs two seconds of work.

  3. 03

    Choose how to sample

    Take one frame every N seconds, sample at a chosen frame rate, spread a fixed number of frames evenly, or capture every single source frame. A live estimate shows the frame count and total size before you commit.

  4. 04

    Export

    Frames stream into the gallery as they encode. Keep the ones you want, then download them individually, bundle the selection as a ZIP, or tile them into a single labelled contact sheet.

03 — Capabilities

A real editor, not a file drop.

The parts that usually get cut from a free web tool: precision, batching, and knowing what you are about to export.

01

Frame-accurate navigation

Step forward and back a single frame at a time using the video’s measured rate, with a filmstrip timeline, hover-preview thumbnails and a millisecond timecode readout. Standard rates like 23.976, 29.97 and 59.94 are detected and snapped, so stepping lands where you expect.

02

Four sampling modes

Interval, target frame rate, fixed count spread evenly across the range, or every source frame. Interval and count modes seek to exact timestamps; every-frame runs capture during playback instead, avoiding a keyframe hunt on each frame.

03

Range in and out points

Draggable, keyboard-adjustable handles on the timeline restrict extraction to a segment. Long recordings stop being all-or-nothing.

04

Format and resolution control

PNG for lossless work, JPEG for size, WebP for both — with a quality slider for the lossy formats. Keep the native resolution or cap the longest edge at 1920, 1280 or 640. FrameByFrame never upscales, and the format list is probed against your browser’s real encoder support rather than assumed.

05

Batch export that scales

Frames stream in as they encode, with progress and a running estimate. Cancel at any point and everything already extracted is kept. Download the lot as a ZIP, or a subset — shift-click selects ranges.

06

Predictable file names

Name by frame number, timecode, or both, zero-padded to the size of the batch so a folder of stills sorts correctly in every file manager.

07

Contact sheets

Tile any selection into one labelled JPEG. Useful for choosing a thumbnail, reviewing coverage, or attaching a visual summary to a ticket.

08

Built for the keyboard

Play, step, jump, mark in and out, capture, select all and start extraction without leaving the keys. Press ? for the full list.

06 — Privacy

The file stays on your machine.

01

No upload step

Your video is read through a local object URL and decoded by the browser. There is no request that carries it anywhere, because there is no backend to receive it.

02

No account, no queue

Nothing to sign up for, no credits, no watermark, and no waiting behind other people’s jobs. Speed depends on your own machine.

03

Extraction works offline

Once the page has loaded, extraction itself needs no network at all. Disconnect and it keeps working — the plainest proof your video is not being sent anywhere.

04

Nothing retained

Frames live in memory for the session. Close the tab and they are gone; only what you explicitly downloaded remains on disk.

05

What we do measure

The page can load Google Analytics for aggregate visit statistics, and Google’s translation widget once you pick a language. Both set cookies and see your IP address, and translation sends the page’s visible text — never your video, and never any extracted frame — to Google. In the EEA, UK, Switzerland and other prior-consent countries nothing loads until you accept; elsewhere you get a notice and a one-click opt-out. Global Privacy Control and Do Not Track are honoured everywhere. Change your mind any time via Privacy choices in the footer.
